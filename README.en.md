# Agent-Building Playbook (English)

<p align="center"><a href="./README.md">简体中文</a> · <b>English</b></p>

> 📖 **Read online:** <https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/>
> (in-repo entry point: [`wiki/index.html`](./wiki/index.html) — or open the local file directly)

A living collection of *patterns* about building better in the agentic age —
*"Programming Pearls, but actionable, for the cognitive-architecture era."*

The theme is **how to build better**, not merely "how to build agents": cognitive
architecture, agent workflow design, context engineering, orchestration, tool
design, and the daily craft of collaborating with AI.

## Inspirations: Sam Schillace

The 113 patterns in this playbook are distilled from a wider community of
practice, and the intellectual thread most worth spelling out traces back to
**Sam Schillace** — co-inventor of Google Docs (its predecessor Writely was his
startup, acquired and renamed by Google), currently Deputy CTO at Microsoft,
a six-time founder with thirty-plus years in Silicon Valley. He wrote a book
about why optimism is an engineering posture, and he keeps writing letters to
practitioners — and to his younger self:

- 📕 **《No Prize for Pessimism》** (2024, 8080 Books / a Microsoft imprint) —
  [Douban book page](https://book.douban.com/subject/37129755/): on why
  optimism, experimentation, and embracing "the messiness of creation" matter
  when building and designing products;
- ✉️ **Sunday Letters** ([sundaylettersfromsam.substack.com](https://sundaylettersfromsam.substack.com/))
  — his newsletter: "How to think like a programmer, letters to my younger
  self, and system thinking";
- 🗂️ **Lessons from Sam Schillace** ([Antoine Buteau's annotated distillation](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-sam-schillace/))
  — an eight-part reading of his frameworks from thirty years at Google / Box /
  Microsoft (the Writely origin story, the Schillace Laws, taste and judgment,
  leadership).

Why elaborate on this here? Because a substantial share of this playbook's
patterns can be read as *operationalizations* of Schillace's ideas: he states
the principles; we break them into executable moves. A thematic mapping
follows.

### 1. Optimism as an engineering posture ("no prize for pessimism")

The book's title is its thesis: **there is no inherent reward for predicting
failure.** Schillace keeps returning to the point that the innovator's stance
is not to enumerate today's constraints ("why this will fail") but to ask
**"What if?" — what happens if the new thing works?** He describes pessimistic
narratives as traps that kill experimentation; innovators must deliberately
return to "the land of possibility and wonder," **make experiments cheap**, and
stay willing to try uncomfortable ideas before they are consensus.

This maps directly onto the playbook's Mindset family:

- **Cheap experiments** → [Build Five to Throw Away](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/build-five-to-throw-away.html)
  (when requirements are unclear, build variants in parallel — comparing them
  reveals what you actually care about) and
  [Implement to Learn](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/implement-to-learn.html)
  (the fastest way to understand a problem is a small throwaway build);
- **Embracing the mess** → [Find the Hard Stuff](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/find-the-hard-stuff.html)
  (the real work is almost never where it looks like it should be — optimism
  is not underestimating difficulty, it is walking straight at it);
- **"What if" over "why not"** → [Agents Amplify Experience](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/agents-amplify-experience.html):
  in an optimist's hands the amplifier magnifies exploration radius; in a
  pessimist's hands it magnifies the cost of defense.

### 2. The Schillace Laws: the division of labor in semantic engineering

His most widely quoted technical framework, the **Schillace Laws**, maps almost
one-to-one onto the playbook's engineering patterns:

| Schillace Law | Playbook pattern |
|---|---|
| **"Code is for syntax and process; models are for semantics and intent."** | [Deterministic Rails](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/deterministic-rails.html): alternate model steps with deterministic steps; neither layer needs to be perfect — the model translates intent into verifiable state, while scripts, tests, and validators catch drift |
| **"Don't write code if the model can do it — the model will get better, but the code won't."** | [Self-Ablating Harness](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/self-ablating-harness.html): periodically let a stronger model re-examine the workflow and delete steps it no longer needs — yesterday's scaffolding is today's tax |
| **"The system will be as brittle as its most brittle part."** | [Reliability Before Features](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/reliability-before-features.html) and [Fail Loud Over Fallbacks](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/fail-loud-over-fallbacks.html): masking fallbacks teach the system nothing and cost as much as the failure |
| **"Uncertainty is an exception throw."** | [Verify Independently](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/verify-independently.html) and [Evidence Before Assertions](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/evidence-before-assertions.html): the model *feeling* done is not done — it is a hypothesis awaiting verification |
| **"Hard for you is hard for the model."** | [Find the Hard Stuff](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/find-the-hard-stuff.html): difficulty does not vanish when delegated to a model; it is merely postponed |
| **"Text is the universal wire protocol."** | [Thin Pointers](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/thin-pointers.html) and [Write, Select, Compress, Isolate](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/write-select-compress-isolate.html): what agents pass between themselves, and how, decides the system's signal-to-noise ratio |
| **"Don't anthropomorphize the model" (beware the "pareidolia of consciousness").** | [Match the Agent Metaphor to the Layer](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/match-agent-metaphor-to-layer.html): treating every agent as a person creates chaos; treating every agent as a stateless tool discards continuity — choose per layer |

### 3. Taste and judgment: the new scarcity after execution becomes free

Part five of Buteau's distillation may be the closest to this playbook's
spirit: **"the coding skills that were rare and valuable thirty years ago are
more or less free now."** When the cost of execution approaches zero — "in a
world where anyone can do *anything*, the thing that matters more is taste and
judgment"; **"the bottleneck has shifted from execution to
decision-making"**; even consuming information requires taste, or you feed
yourself "empty mental calories." His conclusion: **"taste is the one asset
models cannot replicate, because it depends on lived human context and
subjective preference."**

That is precisely the starting point of this playbook's *taste* dimension, and
of several through-lines:

- [Develop Your Taste](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/develop-your-taste.html):
  the ability to recognize quality and sense when something is off must be
  deliberately trained; it does not emerge from piling up output;
- [Attention Is the Scarce Resource](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/attention-is-the-scarce-resource.html):
  when agents drive the cost of output to zero, human attention becomes the new
  budget unit — exactly Schillace's "execution → decision" bottleneck shift;
- [People Edit Better Than They Author](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/people-edit-better-than-they-author.html):
  giving someone a reactable artifact is closer to how taste actually operates
  than asking them to write requirements from a blank page;
- [Finishing Is the Bottleneck](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/finishing-is-the-bottleneck.html):
  models make a passable 80% cheap, so the last 20% — judging whether it is
  actually good — becomes the whole of the value.

### 4. The AI paradigm shift: redesign the work, don't fill humanoid slots

Schillace's read on the current shift: **"AI isn't a feature of your product;
your product is a feature of AI."** He compares generative AI to the web/cloud
transition he lived through — Writely proved the browser could be an
application platform; today he expects software and businesses to be rebuilt
around the new paradigm. He also warns that **"worrying about things you can
fix later, at this moment in time, is an absolutely fatal error"** — speed is
weighted higher than ever — and that "the model's good at thinking, but it's
not good at planning. So you do planning in code."

In playbook terms:

- [Redesign Work, Don't Fill Slots](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/redesign-work-dont-fill-slots.html):
  swapping every role for an equivalent agent just preserves the old
  inefficiencies — isomorphic to "your product is a feature of AI";
- [Leverage Over Efficiency](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/leverage-over-efficiency.html):
  ask "how do the same people build at larger scale," not "how do fewer people
  do the same thing";
- [Workflows vs. Agents](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/workflows-vs-agents.html)
  and [Build From Three Primitives](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/three-primitives.html):
  the concrete practice of "planning in code";
- [Match the Model to the Stage](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/match-model-to-stage.html):
  the right way to trade speed against cost.

### 5. Engineering sobriety and paranoia (the old craft is not obsolete)

Notably, Schillace is no AI-will-do-everything romantic. He carries forward
old-school engineering commandments: **"be paranoid — never trust a system is
up, a bug is fixed, or code works, without rigorous verification"**; **"fix the
possibility of the bug," not just the bug**; **"rewriting from scratch is a
cardinal sin"**; **"simple solutions — even if slightly less complete — usually
win."** Their shadows in this playbook:

- [Evidence Before Assertions](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/evidence-before-assertions.html) = "be paranoid";
- [Fix the Root Cause, with a Diagnosis](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/fix-the-root-cause.html) = "fix the possibility of the bug";
- [Brownfield Is the Real Test](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/brownfield-is-the-real-test.html) = "rewrites are a cardinal sin";
- [Start Least Agentic](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/start-least-agentic.html) = "simple solutions win."

### 6. Leadership: humility, delegation, and coaches vs. players

Another thread of the book is leadership: he warns against the **"prima donna
death spiral"** — leaders who won't delegate drag their teams into
ineffectiveness; effective leadership is rooted in humility and trust. Scaling
means "shifting from primary problem-solver to architect of a problem-solving
culture." On careers, his advice has Silicon Valley wildness in it: **"find the
thing you feel kinda guilty about being paid for, then go hard"**; **"get to
the edge of something and fuck around — that's the strategy."**

Mapped onto the People family:

- [Coaches and Players](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/coaches-and-players.html):
  leverage comes from injecting senior judgment into many agent workflows, not
  from senior people doing senior things by hand — the agentic-era version of
  "from problem-solver to culture architect";
- [Proposer/Authority Separation](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/proposer-authority-separation.html):
  the institutional defense against the prima donna spiral;
- [The New Literacy Is Operating](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/patterns/the-new-literacy-is-operating.html):
  once execution is free, "the thing you feel guilty being paid for" is moving
  from writing code to steering and judging.

### In short

If this playbook is a book of *moves*, Schillace's book and letters supply much
of the *spirit* behind those moves: **set direction with optimism, draw
boundaries with laws, make trade-offs with taste, lead with humility, and
guard quality with paranoia.** Suggested reading order: skim this playbook's
[family home page](https://gotoanywhere2021.github.io/agent-building-playbook/)
to build the map, read *No Prize for Pessimism* for the spirit, then keep
calibrating with Sunday Letters and Buteau's distillation.
